\begin{abstract}
Recorded sales understate demand whenever a product sells out, so models fitted to sales can mislead the ordering decisions they support. We study demand forecasting and newsvendor ordering on FreshRetailNet-50K, a fresh-retail panel with hourly stockout flags. A series-day without a flagged stockout between 06:00 and 21:59 gives an exact, though selected, proxy for demand in that window. Three simulated stockout mechanisms censor these days in different ways, so a model trained on one censored history can be scored on another against the same outcomes. Two independent development runs, on a stratified 5,000-series sample and on all 50,000 series, have nearly equal donor-day shares (0.5580 and 0.5573). A separate end-to-end run on 5,000 series compares nine ordering policies under a protocol locked before the final week was scored. No policy wins under every cost ratio. When a shortage costs three times as much as a leftover unit, LightGBM demand quantiles with pooled conformal calibration give the lowest mean cost among the full-sample policies in every matched history; when leftovers cost more, a raw-sales LightGBM with residual quantiles is cheaper. Evaluating the calibrated policy on a history other than the one it was trained on increases its mean cost by at most 0.0472 normalized units (95\% cluster-bootstrap interval 0.0391--0.0554). For real censored histories, where demand is unobserved, we report only a lower bound on q90 misses.
\end{abstract}
