\section{Data and Experimental Protocol}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Data, outcome, and split}
\label{sec:outcome}

The training file has 4,500,000 series-day rows for 50,000 (city, store, product) series, and the evaluation file has 350,000. The training file holds 865 product identifiers, the number given on the current dataset card~\cite{dingdong2025datasetcard}, while the dataset report states 863 stock-keeping units~\cite{wang2025freshretailnet}. We use release 1.0 (CC BY 4.0). Its upstream revision and our download date were not recorded, so the files are identified by the SHA-256 hashes listed at the end of the paper. The outcome is the recorded sales between 06:00 and 21:59, the window that a next-day order has to cover. A series-day is a donor day when its stock-status flags $s_{j,t,h}$ are all zero over that window, and only then do its window sales serve as the proxy:
\begin{equation}
I^{\mathrm{donor}}_{j,t}=\mathbf{1}\Big\{\sum_{h=6}^{21}s_{j,t,h}=0\Big\},\quad
\Dproxy_{j,t}=\sum_{h=6}^{21}y^{\mathrm{sale}}_{j,t,h}.
\label{eq:donor}
\end{equation}
Donor days are a selected sample. In run~S, the mean recorded sales over the two weeks before a fit-period donor day were 0.91 times those before other days, and donor days fell on promotion days less often (0.35 against 0.39). Real final-week rows with a flagged stockout have no demand label and are never scored for error or cost.

The pipeline runs in ten phases: data checks and sampling (Phase~0), audit and past-only features (1), simulated censoring (2), conventional baselines (3), LightGBM demand quantiles (4), TimesNet and TFT (5), Kaplan--Meier (6), conformal calibration (7), ordering and the protocol lock (8), and the final evaluation (9). All runs use the same chronological split. Days 1--60 (2024-03-28 to 2024-05-26) are used for fitting, days 61--75 (to 2024-06-10) for choosing hyperparameters, and days 76--90 (to 2024-06-25) only for calibration; days 91--97 (2024-06-26 to 2024-07-02) form the final week. The sampling tiers (first-level category, sales tier, and stockout-rate tier) and the initial simulator rules use fit-period data only. Features use only earlier information: target-day flags, sales, weather, and promotions are excluded, lags are shifted by at least one day, and randomizing every target-day field leaves the features unchanged. Until Phase~4 the evaluation file was read only for its schema, identifiers, and dates. Run~E wrote its protocol lock in Phase~8, before any final-week outcome was scored, and the final week was used once.

\subsection{Simulated censoring mechanisms}
\label{sec:mechanisms}

Each mechanism replaces the visible history of eligible donor days (those with enough past data) with a simulated censored one; real censored days keep their recorded values. Under mechanism $m$, the opening stock of series $j$ on day $t$ is
\begin{equation}
Q^{(m)}_{j,t}=\max\!\left\{\mu^{\mathrm{past}}_{j,t}\,k^{(m)}_{j,t}\exp(\sigma_m z_{j,t}),\;q_{\mathrm{floor}}\right\},
\label{eq:simstock}
\end{equation}
where $\mu^{\mathrm{past}}_{j,t}$ is the mean recorded window sales over the previous two weeks, $k^{(m)}_{j,t}$ a stock multiplier fitted to fit-period stockout patterns, $z_{j,t}$ a seeded standard normal draw shared by all mechanisms, and $q_{\mathrm{floor}}$ a small floor. Visible hourly sales are the smaller of proxy sales and remaining stock. Mechanism~A (ordinary depletion) may add one refill of half the opening stock one to three hours after the first simulated stockout, with a probability estimated from real within-day recoveries. Mechanism~B (promotion-sensitive) multiplies A's stock factor by 0.6 on recorded promotion days; the promotion flag drives the simulator but is not used as a forecasting feature. Mechanism~C (sustained scarcity) halves A's multiplier, uses a fixed noise scale, and never refills (Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanisms}). A transfer cell pairs a training mechanism with an evaluation history. Within a cell, every forecast is scored against the same proxy, so cost differences between policies are paired.

\subsection{Models, calibration, ordering, and metrics}
\label{sec:models}

The conventional baselines are a seasonal-naive rule and LightGBM~\cite{ke2017lightgbm} point and quantile models trained on visible sales. The demand-quantile models are LightGBM quantile regressions trained on the donor proxy at $\tau\in\{0.25,0.50,0.75,0.90\}$, one set per mechanism. On a declared deep subset of series, TimesNet recovers artificially hidden hours, and TFT forecasts the next day either from the visible history (direct) or from the TimesNet-recovered one. The Kaplan--Meier policy estimates an order percentile from censored sales within pools that fall back from category by sales tier to category, management group, and finally a global pool. Pooled conformal calibration shifts each quantile by a residual quantile computed on calibration-window donors in the same pool hierarchy (minimum pool size 200) and then sorts each row's corrected quantiles. An adaptive variant~\cite{gibbs2021adaptive} is included as a sensitivity analysis. Point forecasters become quantile forecasts by adding the pooled quantile of their calibration residuals. Table~\ref{tab:policies} lists the nine locked ordering policies built from these models.

With a cost $c_u$ per unit short and $c_o$ per unit left over, the newsvendor order is the $\beta=c_u/(c_u+c_o)$ quantile, and the cost of a row is
\begin{equation}
C(Q,\Dproxy)=c_o\max(Q-\Dproxy,0)+c_u\max(\Dproxy-Q,0),
\label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
evaluated against the unmasked proxy under a one-day shelf life. We lock three settings, $(c_u,c_o,\beta)=(1,3,0.25)$, $(1,1,0.50)$, and $(3,1,0.75)$, and call them excess stock more costly, equal costs, and shortage more costly. Costs are in normalized sales units. On donor rows we also report the weighted absolute percentage error (WAPE) of the q50 forecast, the empirical coverage of each quantile, and the pinball loss~\cite{koenker1978regression}. These measures answer different questions, and we do not rank methods across them. For a real final-week row with recorded window sales $\Sr$ and q90 forecast $U_{90}$, demand is at least $\Sr$, so every row with $\Sr>U_{90}$ is a certain miss. The definite-miss rate
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{miss}}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{1}\{S_{\mathrm{rec},i}>U_{90,i}\}
\label{eq:miss}
\end{equation}
is therefore a lower bound on the real q90 miss rate. It is not a coverage estimate, nor an estimate of censored demand.
