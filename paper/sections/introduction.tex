\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Retailers record sales, not demand. When a product sells out, the record stops at the stock that was available and the unmet part of demand leaves no trace~\cite{fildes2022retail}. A model fitted to such records learns the stocking policy along with customer behavior, and its errors carry into the next order. Fresh retail makes the cost immediate: tomorrow's order trades lost sales against perishable surplus, and a forecast that tracks recorded sales closely can still lead to a poor order if its history was censored~\cite{nahmias1994demand,huh2011adaptive}. A lower forecast error therefore does not by itself show that one ordering policy is better than another. The comparison has to use the same outcome, the same cost ratio, and the same scored rows.

FreshRetailNet-50K~\cite{wang2025freshretailnet,dingdong2025datasetcard} makes this problem measurable because it records an hourly stock-status flag next to hourly sales. A series-day with no flagged stockout between 06:00 and 21:59 is a \emph{donor day}, and its recorded sales over that window are an exact proxy for demand, provided the flags are correct. Donor days are not a random sample: well-stocked and slow-moving items qualify more often, and the proxy says nothing about demand on days that were actually censored. Within that limit, donor days support a controlled experiment. The dataset report uses non-stockout periods in this way to score demand recovery~\cite{wang2025freshretailnet}; we carry the idea from hourly recovery to next-day orders. Three simulated stockout mechanisms censor the histories of eligible donor days in different ways, features and forecasts are rebuilt from each censored history, and every forecast and order is scored against the same uncensored proxy. A model trained under one mechanism can thus be evaluated under another on identical rows.

The evidence comes from three saved executions of one notebook pipeline (Fig.~\ref{fig:lineage}). Two development runs prepare the data, simulate censoring, and tune the models: run~S on a stratified sample of 5,000 series and run~P on all 50,000 series. Run~E, the end-to-end run, repeats these steps on its own 5,000-series sample and continues through neural and censored-inventory baselines, conformal calibration, ordering, a protocol lock, and a single evaluation of the final week. Only run~E feeds the final evaluation.

Our contribution is an evaluation design and the evidence it produces, not a new forecasting architecture. Earlier work on this dataset scores demand recovery and forecast accuracy~\cite{wang2025freshretailnet} or measures profit on censored sales~\cite{azar2026pipeline}. We add the following:
\begin{itemize}
\item we score ordering cost, not only forecast error, against an uncensored proxy, so that nine policies are compared on identical rows with an identified outcome;
\item we vary how the training history was censored and measure the cost of transfer between mechanisms, with paired cluster-bootstrap intervals;
\item we show that the ranking of calibrated demand quantiles and a raw-sales model reverses with the cost ratio in every train/test cell, and we bound the miss rate of the 90\% quantile forecast (q90) on real censored days, where no demand label exists.
\end{itemize}
The code, saved run outputs, and paper sources are public.\footnote{\repourl}

\subsection{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\emph{Censored demand and the newsvendor.} Parametric methods fit a demand distribution to censored observations~\cite{nahmias1994demand}, and nonparametric policies learn order levels from censored sales with the Kaplan--Meier product-limit estimator~\cite{kaplan1958nonparametric,huh2011adaptive}; our Kaplan--Meier baseline follows the second idea with pooled support rules. Theory characterizes how censoring changes what can be learned about the optimal order~\cite{besbes2013implications}, data-driven newsvendor methods have been adapted to censored samples~\cite{sachs2014datadriven}, and treating sales as demand can degrade badly as censored data accumulate~\cite{kumar2026value}; our raw-sales baselines test that heuristic empirically. Feature-based and neural newsvendor methods learn orders directly from covariates~\cite{ban2019bignewsvendor,bertsimas2020predictive,oroojlooyjadid2020deep}; under linear costs the optimal order is a quantile, which ties ordering to quantile regression~\cite{koenker1978regression}.

\emph{Forecasters and calibration.} Our forecasters are gradient-boosted trees~\cite{ke2017lightgbm}, TimesNet~\cite{wu2023timesnet} for recovering masked hours, and the Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT)~\cite{lim2021tft} for next-day forecasts. Tree ensembles are a strong default for retail sales: LightGBM was used by practically all of the top 50 entries in the M5 accuracy competition~\cite{makridakis2022m5}, and simple models often match transformer forecasters~\cite{zeng2023transformers}. Calibration applies pooled split-conformal corrections in the spirit of conformalized quantile regression~\cite{romano2019cqr}, a sequential variant as in adaptive conformal inference~\cite{gibbs2021adaptive}, and monotone rearrangement to remove quantile crossing~\cite{chernozhukov2010quantile}; conformal calibration of the critical quantile has also been proposed for the feature-based newsvendor~\cite{cao2024conformal}. Coverage guarantees rest on exchangeability or on explicit bounds for departures from it~\cite{barber2023conformal}. Real stockout-censored days meet neither condition, so we measure coverage only where the donor proxy exists.

\emph{Evaluation on stockout-annotated data.} The dataset report~\cite{wang2025freshretailnet} introduces the stockout annotations, a two-stage recovery-then-forecast baseline, and a controlled missing-not-at-random simulation that masks synthetic regions of non-stockout periods to score recovery; we reproduced its official code~\cite{dingdong2025baseline} only in part (Section~\ref{sec:deepdev}). Azar~\cite{azar2026pipeline} builds a leakage-free LightGBM pipeline on all 50,000 series and selects among newsvendor policies on a validation fold, but scores profit on censored sales, whose bias relative to demand is not identified. Elsewhere, stockout-aware evaluation excludes out-of-stock days from training losses and accuracy metrics~\cite{lebedev2026stockout}, and a global gradient-boosted model with stockout features feeds a cost-aware ordering rule~\cite{szablowski2026vn2}. None of these scores orders against an uncensored outcome while varying the censoring mechanism, which is the gap our design addresses.
