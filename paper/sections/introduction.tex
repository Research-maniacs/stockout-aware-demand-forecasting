\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Retailers record sales, not demand: when a product sells out, the unmet part of demand leaves no trace~\cite{fildes2022retail}. A model fitted to such records learns the stocking policy along with customer behavior, and in fresh retail its errors carry straight into the next order, which trades lost sales against perishable surplus~\cite{nahmias1994demand,huh2011adaptive}. A lower forecast error therefore does not show that one ordering policy is better than another; the comparison needs the same outcome, the same cost ratio, and the same scored rows.

FreshRetailNet-50K~\cite{wang2025freshretailnet,dingdong2025datasetcard} records an hourly stock-status flag next to hourly sales. A series-day with no flagged stockout between 06:00 and 21:59 is a \emph{donor day}, and its recorded sales over that window are an exact demand proxy, provided the flags are correct. Donor days are not a random sample, and the proxy says nothing about days that were actually censored, but they support a controlled experiment, as in the dataset report's recovery evaluation~\cite{wang2025freshretailnet}. Three simulated stockout mechanisms censor the histories of donor days, features and forecasts are rebuilt from each censored history, and every forecast and order is scored against the same proxy, so a model trained under one mechanism can be evaluated under another on identical rows.

The evidence comes from three saved executions of one notebook pipeline (Fig.~\ref{fig:lineage}). Two development runs prepare the data, simulate censoring, and tune the models: run~S on a stratified sample of 5,000 series and run~P on all 50,000 series. The end-to-end run~E repeats these steps on its own 5,000-series sample and continues through neural and censored-inventory baselines, conformal calibration, ordering, a protocol lock, and a single evaluation of the final week. Only run~E feeds the final evaluation.

Our contribution is an evaluation design and its evidence, not a new forecaster. Earlier work on this dataset scores demand recovery and forecast accuracy~\cite{wang2025freshretailnet} or profit on censored sales~\cite{azar2026pipeline}. We (i)~score ordering cost against an uncensored proxy, comparing nine policies on identical rows; (ii)~vary how the training history was censored and measure the cost of transfer between mechanisms with paired cluster-bootstrap intervals; and (iii)~show that the ranking of calibrated demand quantiles and a raw-sales model reverses with the cost ratio in every train/test cell, and bound the miss rate of the 90\% quantile forecast (q90) on real censored days.

\emph{Related work.} Censored-demand newsvendor methods fit a parametric demand distribution~\cite{nahmias1994demand} or learn order levels nonparametrically with the Kaplan--Meier estimator~\cite{kaplan1958nonparametric,huh2011adaptive}, which our Kaplan--Meier baseline follows. Theory characterizes what censoring hides about the optimal order~\cite{besbes2013implications}, data-driven newsvendor methods have been adapted to censored samples~\cite{sachs2014datadriven}, and treating sales as demand can degrade badly as censored data accumulate~\cite{kumar2026value}. Feature-based and neural newsvendor methods learn orders from covariates~\cite{ban2019bignewsvendor,bertsimas2020predictive,oroojlooyjadid2020deep}; under linear costs the optimal order is a quantile, which ties ordering to quantile regression~\cite{koenker1978regression}. Our forecasters are LightGBM~\cite{ke2017lightgbm}, used by practically all of the top 50 entries in the M5 competition~\cite{makridakis2022m5}, TimesNet~\cite{wu2023timesnet} for recovering masked hours, and the Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT)~\cite{lim2021tft}; simple models often match transformer forecasters~\cite{zeng2023transformers}. Calibration follows conformalized quantile regression~\cite{romano2019cqr}, adaptive conformal inference~\cite{gibbs2021adaptive}, and monotone rearrangement~\cite{chernozhukov2010quantile}, and conformal calibration has been proposed for the feature-based newsvendor~\cite{cao2024conformal}; its guarantees rest on exchangeability or bounds on departures from it~\cite{barber2023conformal}, which real censored days do not meet. On this dataset, the report~\cite{wang2025freshretailnet} scores recovery on synthetically masked non-stockout periods with an official baseline~\cite{dingdong2025baseline} that we reproduced only in part, and Azar~\cite{azar2026pipeline} selects newsvendor policies but scores profit on censored sales, whose bias is not identified. Elsewhere, stockout-aware evaluation excludes out-of-stock days~\cite{lebedev2026stockout} or feeds a cost-aware ordering rule~\cite{szablowski2026vn2}. None of these scores orders against an uncensored outcome while varying the censoring mechanism.
