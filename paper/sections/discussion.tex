\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

The reversal with the cost ratio follows the direction of the censoring bias. Sales understate demand, which costs little when the order targets a low quantile and much when it targets a high one, so a model trained on recorded sales does well when excess stock is the expensive error and falls behind when shortage is. This qualifies the warning that treating sales as demand degrades as censored data accumulate~\cite{kumar2026value}: here the penalty appears when shortage is costly.

The ablations recorded in run~E (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}) agree with the locked comparisons: averaged over the matched histories at equal costs, the pooled-conformal demand quantiles cost 0.3984 against 0.4075 for the raw-sales point model with residual quantiles. Pooled calibration moved q90 coverage from 0.8121 to 0.8832 against a nominal 0.90. The adaptive variant cost 0.4039 against 0.3984 for static calibration, so its drift correction did not pay off in this week. A hypothetical two-day carry-over of unsold stock lowered the cost from 0.3984 to 0.2579, which shows how much the cost level depends on the one-day shelf-life assumption. Giving the model the target-day weather and promotion values, which are unknown when the order is placed, changed the q50 WAPE only from 33.48\% to 33.40\%. A reduced-lag ablation was not run, because fitting a new model after the final week had been scored would fall outside the lock.

The main limitations are the following.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Proxy and mechanisms.} Donor-day sales equal demand only on complete days, which depend on demand and stocking, so the proxy does not identify demand on censored days, where we give only a lower bound. Mechanisms A, B, and C are specified simulations, not counterfactual inventory paths; transfer results measure sensitivity to these histories, not transport to another retailer or stocking policy.
\item \emph{Populations and neural models.} Kaplan--Meier scores only estimable rows. The neural policies cover the 600-series subset with a small CPU and epoch budget, and the official baseline was reproduced only in part, so they are reference points; a fair comparison needs a full training budget and the same scored rows as the tree-based policies.
\item \emph{Cost model.} Rankings depend on the locked cost ratios and on the one-day shelf life.
\item \emph{One evaluated week.} Results are stable across its seven dates (Section~\ref{sec:robust}), but one week cannot show seasonal effects, and any later protocol evaluated on it is exploratory. The 45,000 series outside run~E's sample have never been scored, so a fresh locked replication on them is possible.
\item \emph{Intervals and provenance.} The intervals use only 200 bootstrap replicates, do not resample dates separately, and have no multiplicity adjustment. No run hashed the notebook source it executed, and the upstream dataset revision and download date are unknown, so the local file hashes are the operative identifiers. All runs used Linux, Python 3.11.15, two logical CPUs, and 8.42~GB of RAM, run~E without a GPU. The released outputs omit large intermediate files, so Phase~5 cannot resume from them without regenerating Phases~0--4.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A matched donor-proxy design makes stockout-aware forecasting and ordering comparisons on FreshRetailNet-50K checkable: models trained on differently censored histories are scored against the same outcomes, under a protocol fixed before the final week was read. Within this design, the value of calibrated demand quantiles depends on the cost ratio. They gave the lowest cost in every train/test cell when shortage was more costly, while a raw-sales point model with residual quantiles was cheaper in every cell when excess stock was more costly. Evaluating the calibrated policy on an unseen mechanism raised its cost by at most 0.0472 normalized units, the smallest worst case among the policies that read the evaluation history, and TimesNet-recovered histories lowered TFT order costs within the deep subset. These findings hold for donor days, the simulated mechanisms, and the locked cost settings. Testing them on censored demand itself requires a new protocol; testing them on the 45,000 unscored series requires only a new lock.
