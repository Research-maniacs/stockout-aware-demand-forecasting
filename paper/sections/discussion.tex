\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The reversal with the cost ratio follows the direction of the censoring bias. Sales understate demand, which costs little when the order targets a low quantile and much when it targets a high one, so a model trained on recorded sales does well when excess stock is the expensive error and falls behind when shortage is. This qualifies the warning that treating sales as demand degrades as censored data accumulate~\cite{kumar2026value}: here the penalty appears when shortage is costly. The ablations recorded in run~E agree. Averaged over the matched histories at equal costs, the pooled-conformal demand quantiles cost 0.3984 against 0.4075 for the raw-sales point model; the adaptive variant cost 0.4039, so its drift correction did not pay off in this week; a hypothetical two-day carry-over of unsold stock lowers the cost to 0.2579, which shows how much the cost level depends on the one-day shelf life; and target-day weather and promotion values, unknown when the order is placed, change the q50 WAPE only from 33.48\% to 33.40\%.

\emph{Limitations.} Donor-day sales equal demand only on complete days, which depend on demand and stocking, so the proxy does not identify demand on censored days, where we give only a lower bound. Mechanisms A, B, and C are specified simulations, not counterfactual inventory paths, so transfer results measure sensitivity to these histories, not transport to another retailer. Kaplan--Meier scores only estimable rows, and the neural policies are budget-limited references on 600 series. Rankings depend on the locked cost ratios and the one-day shelf life, and one evaluated week cannot show seasonal effects; any later protocol evaluated on it is exploratory, while the 45,000 series outside run~E's sample have never been scored. The intervals use only 200 replicates. No run hashed the notebook source it executed, and the upstream dataset revision and download date are unknown, so the local file hashes are the operative identifiers. All runs used Linux, Python 3.11.15, two logical CPUs, and 8.42~GB of RAM, run~E without a GPU.

\emph{Conclusion.} A matched donor-proxy design makes stockout-aware forecasting and ordering comparisons on FreshRetailNet-50K checkable: models trained on differently censored histories are scored against the same outcomes, under a protocol fixed before the final week was read. Calibrated demand quantiles gave the lowest cost in every train/test cell when shortage was more costly, and a raw-sales point model with residual quantiles did so when excess stock was more costly. Evaluating the calibrated policy on an unseen mechanism raised its cost by at most 0.0472 normalized units. These findings hold for donor days, the simulated mechanisms, and the locked cost settings; testing them on censored demand requires a new protocol, and testing them on the 45,000 unscored series requires only a new lock.
